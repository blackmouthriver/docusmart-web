How to convert a PDF to Word to edit it, even if it's scanned

Someone sent you a PDF and you need to change a date, a name or a paragraph, but you don't have the original Word file. Here is how to get it back as an editable document.

4 min readUpdated on November 26, 2026

Before converting: what to expect

A PDF doesn't store paragraphs or tables: only letters and their position on the page. That's why any conversion to Word rebuilds the document, and the result looks very much like the original, but isn't identical. PDF to Word keeps:

  • Headings, bold, italics, font sizes and colors.
  • Lists, simple tables and images.
  • Two-column layouts, like the ones in résumés, with the background of each column.

Text boxes and complex decorative shapes may come out differently. It works best with PDFs created from Word or a similar program.

Step by step

Open PDF to Word and choose the file. If the PDF is scanned, or has some scanned pages, leave Recognize text on scanned pages ticked and pick the document's language: those pages are read with OCR in the same step and their text becomes editable.

Press Convert. You will see an approximate preview of the result next to the original, with how many paragraphs and images came out. Download the .docx and open it in Word, Google Docs, LibreOffice, Pages or WPS.

Check the document when you open it, especially tables and page breaks: that's where the layout tends to vary most.

Do you need Word, or is editing the PDF enough?

If you are only going to change a word, add a date or cover up a detail, you don't need to go through Word: with Edit PDF you write on top of the PDF and it stays just as it was, with its exact layout. Converting to Word makes sense when you are going to rewrite whole paragraphs or reuse the content in another document.

Back to PDF

When you finish editing, save the document and convert it back with Word to PDF to send it. That way whoever receives it sees it the same on any device, regardless of the fonts they have installed.

Your document stays on your device

The conversion, and the OCR of scanned pages, happen in your browser: the PDF isn't uploaded to any server. It works just as well for contracts, résumés or work documents that shouldn't be passed around.

Quick summary

  1. Open PDF to Word. Go to docusmart.online/en/pdf-to-word and choose the PDF.
  2. Turn on OCR if needed. If it has scanned pages, leave the OCR box ticked and pick the language.
  3. Convert. Press Convert and check the approximate preview.
  4. Download and edit. Download the .docx and open it in Word, Google Docs or LibreOffice.

Frequently asked questions

Does it come out identical to the PDF?

Very close, but not identical: the document is rebuilt from the position of the letters. Headings, formatting, lists, simple tables, images and two columns are kept.

Does it work with scanned PDFs?

Yes. With the OCR box ticked, scanned pages are recognized in your browser and their text becomes editable.

Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

If it only has restrictions (it opens without a password), yes. If it asks for a password to open, remove it first with Unlock PDF.
