How to convert a PDF to PDF/A for an official filing or for archiving

A court, a university or a government agency asks for the document "in PDF/A" and the portal rejects your PDF. Here is how to convert it, and how to know which version to pick.

4 min readUpdated on November 26, 2026

What PDF/A is and why they ask for it

PDF/A is the version of PDF designed for archiving (ISO 19005). The file carries everything it needs to display inside it: its fonts, its color profile and its metadata, and it can't depend on anything external, such as scripts, links to fonts or content loaded from somewhere else. That way it looks the same today and many years from now.

That's why courts ask for it when filing briefs and lawsuits, many universities for theses and dissertations, and government agencies for applications and records.

Which version to choose

  • PDF/A-2b: the recommended one, and the one almost every institution asks for. It supports transparency and layers.
  • PDF/A-1b: the oldest, only for systems that accept nothing else. It doesn't support transparency or layers.
  • PDF/A-3b: like 2b, but it keeps attached files, for example the XML of an e-invoice.

If the filing doesn't say which one, use PDF/A-2b.

Step by step

Open PDF to PDF/A, choose the PDF and the Version, and press Convert to PDF/A. When it finishes you will see the result next to the original and a summary of what was adjusted to meet the standard: for example, the fonts that were embedded in the file or the scripts that were removed.

Files created by DocuSmart are validated with veraPDF, the PDF Association's reference validator, the same one many portals use to check what they receive.

If a page was saved as an image

Some things can't go into PDF/A as they are: print colors (CMYK) without their profile, or fonts that aren't inside the file and can't be embedded. In that case that page is saved as an image: it looks the same, but its text can no longer be selected. DocuSmart tells you which pages and why. If you need that text to be searchable, run the result through OCR: recognize text.

If the portal also sets a size limit, check the file size afterwards and, if needed, use Compress PDF.

Your document stays on your device

The conversion happens in your browser: the PDF isn't uploaded to any server. You can convert lawsuits, contracts or ID documents without leaving copies anywhere else.

Quick summary

  1. Open PDF to PDF/A. Go to docusmart.online/en/pdf-to-pdfa and choose the PDF.
  2. Choose the version. PDF/A-2b if the filing doesn't say which; 1b or 3b only if they ask for them.
  3. Convert. Press Convert to PDF/A and check the summary of what was adjusted.
  4. Download. Download the PDF/A and upload it to the filing portal.

Frequently asked questions

Which version do courts ask for?

Almost always PDF/A-2b, or simply "PDF/A". If the institution names a version, choose that one.

Does the content of my document change?

Not the text or the layout. Missing fonts are embedded and things the standard doesn't allow are removed, such as scripts or attachments (except in PDF/A-3b).

How do I know it meets the standard?

When it finishes, DocuSmart tells you which version it meets. Results are validated with veraPDF, the reference validator.
