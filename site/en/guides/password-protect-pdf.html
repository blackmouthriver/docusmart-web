How to password-protect a PDF

A PDF with your personal, financial or medical information that travels by email or chat can end up anywhere. Adding a password takes a minute and keeps the wrong people from opening it.

4 min readUpdated on November 18, 2026

When it makes sense to protect a PDF

  • ID documents, bank statements, pay stubs or tax returns.
  • Medical records, test results or sick notes.
  • Contracts or proposals you don't want circulating.
  • Any file you're sending through a channel you don't control, such as an email that gets forwarded or a group chat.

Step by step

Open Protect PDF with a password and choose the document. Type the password twice.

Decide what is allowed once the PDF is open: printing, copying text or modifying it. Tap "Protect PDF" and the encrypted version downloads. It opens in Adobe Acrobat, Chrome, Edge and the DocuSmart app, which ask for the password.

A good password and how to share it

  • Use at least 10 characters; a short phrase with numbers is easy to remember and hard to guess.
  • Don't use your ID number or your date of birth: that's the first thing someone would try.
  • Never send the password through the same channel as the PDF. If the file goes by email, send the password by text message or say it over the phone.

Keep the password somewhere safe. If you forget it, it can't be recovered: not even DocuSmart can open the PDF, because the password never leaves your device.

What each option protects

The open password is the real protection: without it, the encrypted file can't be read. Permissions (print, copy, modify) are something else: they tell PDF readers what to allow once the document is open.

Reputable programs respect those permissions, but someone who already has the password can remove them with certain tools. Use them to prevent accidental copying or casual edits, not as your only barrier. If someone shouldn't see a piece of information, don't give them the password, or redact that information before sending the file.

A password is not the same as redacting

A password stops people from opening the file, but whoever has it will see all the content. If you need to share a document while hiding only certain details (an account number, an address), use Redact PDF: it actually deletes the text underneath the covered areas, instead of just placing a black box on top.

Removing the password later

If you later need an unprotected copy, Unlock PDF removes the password, as long as you know it. Only use it with your own documents or ones you have the right to modify.

Quick summary

  1. Open Protect PDF. Go to the Protect PDF tool on docusmart.online and choose the document.
  2. Type the password. Type it twice to confirm it.
  3. Choose the permissions. Check whether printing, copying or modifying is allowed.
  4. Protect and download. Tap "Protect PDF" and the encrypted document downloads.

Frequently asked questions

How secure is it?

It uses AES-256 encryption, the strongest standard for PDFs. The real security depends on the password being hard to guess.

Is my password sent to a server?

No. Encryption happens in your browser; neither the file nor the password leaves your device.

Can I remove the password from someone else's PDF?

Only if you know the password. DocuSmart doesn't crack open passwords.
